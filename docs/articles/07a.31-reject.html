  1. The intonation contours which conditioned the stress posi­ tion in these forms were those of the form of the biblical reading tradition which gave rise to the stress and vowel pat­ terns. This reading tradition was intended to express the meaning of the text; consequently it reflects syntactic and other semantic relationships. Where a verb form with vocalic affix and originally short penultimate syllable is not related to what follows (terminal position, a 4pausal’ situation), stress is I penultimate. Where the form is related to what follows (medial | position, a 4contextual’ situation), stress is final. Stress position in the forms to be discussed here is determined in exactly the same way, save that the development of final stress in these forms requires a particularly close relationship to what follows.

It is important to note that the received accentuation arose 3. in a form of the reading tradition somewhat different from that reflected by vowelling and stressing of the text, so that the accentuation does not necessarily reflect the relationships which determined stress position. However the two forms of reading tradition were obviously closely related, so that the accentua­ tion has value as corroborative evidence (see NA § 1.15). The relationships which determined stress position in the biblical text can only be described in terms of clausal syntax, or of features which show relationship between clauses, as particles, or cross-referent pronouns (see NA §2.5). Such relationships could affect stress position within a speech unit, but not across its boundaries. Speech units were delimited by the intonation contours which give rise to standard pausal forms. Relatively few words appear in distinct pausal and contextual forms, however, so that the accentuation must be used as a guide to the probable boundaries of these units. Pausal forms almost invariably occur with the accents silluq and atndh, and (in the Three Books) *olah we-yored, so that these accents can be taken as marking the end of speech units (although about 20 per cent of pausal forms do not occur with them). The units terminated by these accents are called 4major units’ (see NA § 1.19.11).2
