1 Introduction

Biblical Hebrew has been subject to much more intensive study than the Ethiopian languages which Prof. Leslau has done so much to elucidate. However, its largely practical aims (pedagogy, and the justification or improvement of translations) have led to a tendency to stress the similarity in the function of different structures. If Hebrew uses several structures, but modern languages use only one, the Hebrew structures are often seen as synonymous.1 Concentration on Hebrew usage, on the differences in contexts in which different structures occur rather than on their similarities, can often improve our understanding of the semantics of grammatical categorization, and so lead to better translation. This paper attempts this in the field of conditional sentences, where those introduced by are recognized as a separate group, but those introduced by יכ and םא, and some other structures, have not been differentiated. The study is restricted to prose texts, where the conditions governing the use of these structures are most easily appreciated.

You can go... and drink...’ Where translation as a conditional is suitable (which is not true for all similar pairs), the protasis represents a known or accepted fact, a ‘given’. Similarly, תאז ויחו (Gen 42:18, with imperative verbs) can be translated ‘If you do this you will live’, but its real meaning is much more positive. In 1 Kgs 21:2 ‘Give me (imperative) your vineyard... and I will give...’ could equally well be translated ‘If you give me... I will give...’, but this is a demand, and quite different from the mere offer of alternative compensation presented in the following םא clause ‘Or if you like...’ (with adjective or stative verb cf. 21:6). 9.1 The types of conditional sentence discussed above can be divided into two categories: those in which the condition presented by the protasis is hypothetical, and those in which it is actual. Each can be further subdivided: under hypothetical, the condition may be invalid (it cannot occur), or it may be potential (its validity is not known).
