STRESS AND THE WAW “CONSECUTIVE” IN BIBLICAL HEBREW E. J. REVELL UNIVERSITY OF TORONTO
[Page 437]
1 2

The stress patterns of biblical Hebrew are most easily explained as derived from a stage in which words ending in a consonant had final stress, words ending in a vowel, penultimate. Consequently, stress position in the great majority of verb forms with waw consecutive—perfect and imperfect—must have been the same, at this stage, as in the corresponding forms in other uses, and in most cases the forms would have been identical. Suggestions are made as to how the marks now distinguishing waw consecutive forms arose, and as to the historical context in which such marks became necessary.

  • 1. THE NATURE AND ORIGIN OF waw CONSECUTIVE FORMS IN HEBREW are a perennial problem. Attempts to find a solution always involve stress patterns, but these are invariably regarded as the result of a historical process, not as part of an existing system. Consequently a part of the information which they convey is ignored.

2. The stress patterns of biblical Hebrew can be described as follows:

2.1 A word ending in a consonant has final stress, as dā'ḇār, daḇā'rîm, qā'ṭal, qəṭal'tem , yiq'ṭōl, wayyiq'ṭōlūn.

EXCEPT (i) Segolate form nouns and corresponding structures, as ke'sep , se'per, mamle'ket, ḥe'rep , wayyi'ben, bəna'yik .

(ii) Some waw consecutive imperfect forms with open penultimate syllable (as described in #10.1), also hiššā'mēr (see note 19), in contextual position.

(iii) 3fs perfect verb forms with 2fs or 3mpl suffix, as gənāḇā'tam (Gen 31:32), šōḇā'ḇtek (Is 47:10).

2.2 A word ending in a vowel has final stress if it derives from a root III ʾ, or (except for the forms mentioned in #2.3) III h, as gālô', yiglô', wayyeš'e', gālû', rā'ê, bārā', nāśā', or where the final vowel represents a suffixed pronoun, the fs nominal ending, or the “gentilic” ending, or a similar ending in a few other nouns, as dəḇārô' , dəḇārî', šānā', ʿazzā'tî, napta'lî.

2.3 A word ending in a vowel also has final stress in (other) forms ending in -CāCV. These occur in contextual position only. Examples are verb forms ending in a vowel representing a pronominal element, as qāṭā'lû, tiqṭə'lû, yiqṭə'lû, and some words from root III h: nouns of the form pərî', ḥŏlî', and the corresponding verb forms as yaḥ'î, yah'î, also some pronominal morphs, as ʾănî', hinnənî' , dəḇārakā'.

EXCEPT forms in which -āh “directional” is bound to a noun originally ending -CVCC, as ʾō'hĕlā also lā'yəlā.
