3.  — In order to clarify the point of view from which this
study is written, a short account of the syntactical structure of
the Hebrew language as here envisaged must be given. Most
Hebrew grammars are written in terms of two levels of structure,
‘words’, and *clauses’. However a much more exact and detailed
analysis can be carried out if further levels of structure are isolated
for individual investigation. It is well known that words are
formed of smaller units, known as morphemes (5). Morphemes
are the basic units of syntactic structure. They combine to form
the next larger unit, the word. The term ‘word’ is used here
with the meaning ‘member of a grammatical word class’ (6). Word
classes which can be most easily distinguished are: verb (v),
noun (n) (7), pronoun, infinitive (i) (8), article (h), conjunction (c),
and preposition (p). The remaining particles are more difficult
to classify. The outline of a new scheme, with reasons for rejecting
the old, is given below in § 7.

20.  — The two parts of clauses of this type are usually called
‘subject’ and ‘predicate’. It is, however, possible to justify the
use of these terms in less than 25% of the clauses. The use of
such terms to denote grammatical units is only justifiable if it
can be shown that the units are distinguished by some grammatical
marker. Thus the ‘predicate’ of a verbal clause is distinguished
by the fact that it contains the verb. Where no such grammatical
marker exists, it is obvious that the grounds on which ‘subject’
is distinguished from ‘predicate’ cannot be grammatical. They
cannot, therefore, be treated as grammatical units.

21.  — As type 1 shows, it is not necessary that either part
of a two part nominal clause be an exponent of N (46). Conse­
quently, even where one part of the clause does consist of an expo­
nent of N, as in clause types 2-6, it is not necessarily the sub­
ject (47). Consequently one must admit that in clause types 1 to 6,
the grammatical structure of the clauses provides no means of
distinguishing subject and predicate.
