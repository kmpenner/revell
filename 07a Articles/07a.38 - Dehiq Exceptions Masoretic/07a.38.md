1.  Dehiq typically occurs in the first letter of a word of
which the first full vowel is stressed, and which follows a word
ending in unstressed segol or qames, where the two are joined
by maqqef or by a conjunctive accent. It does not occur where
the letter in question is ww, ,alef, he, het, or ‘ayin, (but it does
occur in resh). Where the unstressed vowel at the end of the
preceding word is followed by ,alef, dehiq either does not
occur, or is extremely rare, in biblical Hebrew.  Consequently, a
pair of words joined by maqqef or a conjunctive accent is
called a “standard pair” for the occurrence of dehiq where the
first word ends in unstressed qames or segol which is not
followed by ,alef, and the second begins with a letter other
than waw, ,alef, he, het or *ayin, and is stressed on its first full
vowel. For purposes of this study, where the first word of a

4.4

It seems clear, then, that, in group 2.ii, the occurrence
of dehiq is conditioned by the form of the second word, and by
its relationship to the first. It appears that the occurrence of
dehiq is inhibited where the second word is “long” in that the
stressed vowel is followed by another vowel (#4.2), or preceded
by a consonant with shewa (#4.3). It also seems probable that it
is inhibited where the initial consonant of the second word is
resh. On the question of relationship, the fact that a word of
form CVCV always shows dehiq where it is a preposition with
a pronominal suffix, but not always where it is a noun, suggests
that syntactic/semantic relationship of the sort which affects the
occurrence of nesiga was significant here also. It is assumed (as
with nesiga) that it was prosodic patterns reflecting these
relationships which actually conditioned the use of dehiq* This
suggests, then, that the occurrence of dehiq is determined by a
combination of phonotactic and prosodic features, as has been
shown to be the case for other types of sound variation.  The

9.  The necessity of mentioning shifts in stress position in
the definition of group 2.ii of the standard pattern shows that
dehiq has implications for the history of the language. These
cannot be explored in full, but their importance can be
illustrated by one point. Dehiq occurs consistently after 2m.s
perfect forms with waw consecutive where these have maqqef,
or a conjunctive accent with penultimate stress, just as it does
after the same forms with no prefixed conjunction. That is, in
the matter of dehiq, waw consecutive perfect forms act as
members of group 2.ii. In the other forms in this sub-group, the
penultimate syllable, which was open with a short vowel, was
stressed until a period relatively recent in the history of the
Tiberian tradition, as is well known. The fact that 2m^ perfect
