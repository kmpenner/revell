1 Introduction

Biblical Hebrew has been subject to much more intensive study than the Ethiopian
languages which Prof. Leslau has done so much to elucidate. However, its largely
practical aims (pedagogy, and the justification or improvement of translations) have
led to a tendency to stress the similarity in the function of different structures. If
Hebrew uses several structures, but modern languages use only one, the Hebrew
structures are often seen as synonymous.1 Concentration on Hebrew usage, on the
differences in contexts in which different structures occur rather than on their
similarities, can often improve our understanding of the semantics of grammatical
categorization, and so lead to better translation. This paper attempts this in the
field of conditional sentences, where those introduced by

structures, have not been differentiated. The study is restricted to prose texts,
where the conditions governing the use of these structures are most easily
appreciated.

9.1  The types of conditional sentence discussed above can be divided into two
