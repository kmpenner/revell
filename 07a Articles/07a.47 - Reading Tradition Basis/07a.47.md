5.  These illustrations do not represent a fixed practice, but they do show the importance of

the spoken form as a basis for categorization. Anyone used to reading masoretic notes will

be well aware that the spoken form is used in this way, but the fact is worth emphasis for two

reasons. Biblical scholars tend to regard the stabilization of the reading tradition as the final

stage in the development of the masoretic text. This is no doubt true for some details, but the

extensive use of the spoken form as a basis for notes on spelling shows that the reading

tradition played a major part in the effort to maintain a stable text. The stabilization of the

reading tradition must, then, have occurred at an early period, as I have argued elsewhere2.

The second reason for emphasizing the importance of the spoken form as a basis for masoretic

notes is that this function is sometimes overlooked by masoretic scholars. The remainder of

the paper attempts to show how the concept may help to further the understanding of the

methods of the Masoretes, and so help to elucidate obscure notes.

CONCLUSION

15.  If this reconstruction is correct, then, the MM and MP of L were designed to provide

complete information on the spelling of this confusing set of forms in abstract, complete

information on the use of waw to represent holemy and almost complete information on the

spelling of forms with a prefixed conjunction. Similar use of the MP to refer to the basic

information on spelling provided in the MM, but not to all the information, can also be seen

in the note on םיאישנ. MM 1367 (at Josh 22:14) gives a complete description of the spelling.

This must be considered to be based on the spoken form, as it includes examples conventional-

ly assigned to different words. The MP refers to the two basic categories of spelling, but (as

with the notes on the f. pl. imperfect qal forms of אוב) not to the catchall groups, nor to

unique spellings. Instead, it notes three cases where the word /nesi3’im/ is joined to a

preceding word with maqqef, forming a unique combination; it notes the variant spellings of

the two cases where /nesi3’im/ has both the article and the conjunction prefixed, and it notes

that in four cases the word represents the semantic field of «cloud». Taken together, the MM

and MP in such cases provide as much information as possible on the word in question. This

seems to me to represent a common practise, and so a further argument for the correctness

of my reconstruction of list [6], dealing with /täb’onä/. Whether or no that list did, in origin,

refer to the spoken form, however, it is clear that many masoretic notes are based on the

reading tradition, not on the written text. The understanding of this is important to the full

appreciation of the purpose and methods of the masoretes.

In the note in the MP of Leningrad on ךתר^א in Ps 138:2,

 «twelve times,

and haser», the statement haser is superfluous as a reference to the spelling, as the word never

letter, but is not, that is, to the segol which marks the form as pausal. Such notes indicate the

introduction of vowel signs. The paper gives examples of other notes which are best

understood as based on the spoken form of the word, and discusses cases where this concept

can be used to elucidate notes which appear confused or inconsistent.
